Your movement profile

Scored 0–5 from a short running-gait clip. Re-filmed at the taper to show your progress.

How to film your clip (about 10 min)

Wear running shoes, fitted leggings/shorts and a fitted top (so hips, knees, ankles show), hair tied back.

Phone on slow-motion if easy (iPhone: Camera → Slo-mo; Android: Camera → More → Slow motion). Hold it sideways, wipe the lens, good light, not toward a window.

Who holds it: prop it at hip height, or hand it to your child — "both hands, keep Mummy in the middle, stay still, count slowly to twenty."

Two angles, ~15–20 s each:

1. Side — camera level and square, hip height, whole body in frame; one side then the other.

2. Behind — centred right behind you, level, hip height (hips down to heels).

Mark the spot (a tile or tape) so your taper re-film sits in the same place. Safety: settle at the pace first, never look down at the phone while running.

Did you fuel the work today?

Fuel follows the day. A big session needs plenty; an easy, recovery or unwell day needs less — and that's right, not a miss. Pick today first; the "enough" mark moves to match.

On long runs

Past about 60 minutes, aim for roughly 30–60 g of carbs an hour — about one gel every 30–45 min, or chews/drink to match. Short easy runs don't need it.

The steady base — fat & fibre

Carbs are what we move up and down with the training — that's the bottle. Fat, protein and fibre are the steady base: they barely flex, and they're never the thing to cut. Periodised fuelling flexes the carbs, not the base.

After, and away from it

Bring fibre and healthy fats back from your next proper meal on — veg, wholegrains, good fats. Away from the session is where they belong.

Why fat stays in

Hormones, bone and menstrual health, and vitamins A, D, E, K all ride on dietary fat. Keep it steady; don't let it fall when carbs drop.

Why fibre stays in

Your gut needs it daily. Cutting it to feel "light" backfires — it's timing, not removal. Keep fibre in; just don't stack it against your hardest runs.
